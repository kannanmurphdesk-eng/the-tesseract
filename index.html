About the Book

This book is an 11-chapter magic pill box, one idea each, built for the aspirant who wants clarity. No gimmicks. No overnight millionaire mantras. No early morning robotic rituals. Just a mystery bag of sharp, pragmatic solutions.

You're not lazy. You're not lost. You're just carrying too many unfinished things at once, and no one taught you how to put them down. Every decision, every “should I or shouldn't I,” sits open in the background, quietly draining you before the day even starts.

This book is like a wiper for a windshield. It's going to clear the fog and help its aspirants get a clear view on their journey. This book won't decide the path. It will give light so the aspirant can walk their own, with confidence and clarity.

A system with clarity on both the problem and the desired outcome has 80% solved its purpose.

Past choices, present moment, future consequences: the three layers.

You'll say things like…

  • “Oh my god, how did I miss this simple thing?”
  • “This one small change will make a big impact. Let me do this.”
  • “How did I miss this approach for this problem?”
  • “Yes, fatigue isn't the problem. The way I'm dealing with it is.”
  • “Wow, this is spot on. Let me fix this myself.”
  • “Gotcha, this is the missing piece of my life. Thank you.”

An interrogative, interesting reading journey. Author and reader searching together, not a guide handing down pre-solved answers. It is the journey of Kannan Mohanan and his findings, alongside his editor, Zendya Copeland.

About the Author

Kannan Mohanan

An engineer with more than a decade of experience in instrumentation business and sales, he has dealt with multinationals, including Japanese, American, and European professionals, as well as people from different ethnic groups across the Indian subcontinent.

He is fascinated by how successful people handle their chaos, what different ways of approach and prioritisation strategies they follow, and how these differ from person to person.

He is trying to uncover simple words like necessary, dharma, focus, priority, and meaningful. These words are very simple to say, but they carry a whole lot of weight in terms of the magic they hold.
